XL Reinsurance Pricing Actuarial Framework

Mathematical foundations for non-proportional treaty pricing, burning cost scaling, and volatility margin allocations.

1. Non-Proportional Layer Mechanics

An Excess of Loss (XL) reinsurance treaty is structured as Limit xs Deductible (e.g., $C \text{ xs } D$). For any annual aggregate claim payout $X$, the reinsurer pays:
$$Y = \min\left(\max(X - D, 0), \; C\right)$$ Where $D$ is the attachment point (deductible) and $C$ is the layer capacity (cover limit).

2. Burning Cost with Volatility Loading

Rather than relying solely on pure burning cost (expected payout $\mathbb{E}[Y]$), reinsurance actuaries apply standard deviation loading to compensate for volatility and capital cost:
$$\text{Layer Rate} = \mathbb{E}[Y] + \lambda \times \sigma(Y)$$ Where $\lambda$ is the stdev_loading scalar factor (typically 0.15 to 0.35 depending on market hardening).

3. Treaty Scaling Factor

The contractual attachment bands (e.g. 150–175%) are expressed relative to the Subject Premium. The scaling factor is calculated from the reference Premium Burn-Rate Amount and the target Loss Ratio ($\text{LR}$):
$$\text{Scale} = \frac{\mathbb{E}[X] + \lambda \times \sigma(X)}{\text{LR} / 100}$$ For each specified layer $[l_i, u_i]$:
$$D_i = \text{Scale} \times \frac{l_i}{100}, \quad C_i = \text{Scale} \times \frac{u_i}{100} - D_i$$

4. Rate on Line (ROL %)

Rate on Line measures the annual reinsurance premium as a percentage of the layer limit:
$$\text{ROL} = \left(\frac{\text{Layer Rate}}{C_i}\right) \times 100\%$$ Higher ROL signifies that the layer is lower down in the tower (attaching closer to the expected loss) or has higher exceedance probability.
